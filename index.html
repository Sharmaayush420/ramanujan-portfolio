01 / THE STORY

About Ramanujan

“Mathematics was not just a subject for Ramanujan; it was a language through which he saw patterns hidden in the world.”

Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887–1920) was an Indian mathematician whose extraordinary intuition led to important discoveries in number theory and mathematical analysis. Born in Erode and raised in Kumbakonam, he developed a deep interest in mathematics from an early age. Much of his knowledge came through independent study, including a book of mathematical results that he explored intensely.

Although he had limited formal university education, Ramanujan produced original formulas and theorems that attracted the attention of mathematicians in India and England. In 1913, he began corresponding with G. H. Hardy of Cambridge University. Their collaboration brought Ramanujan to Trinity College, Cambridge, where he continued his research and earned a Bachelor of Arts by Research in 1916. His work covered infinite series, continued fractions, elliptic functions, and integer partitions. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1918. Ramanujan died in 1920, aged 32, but his notebooks and ideas continue to inspire mathematical research around the world.
